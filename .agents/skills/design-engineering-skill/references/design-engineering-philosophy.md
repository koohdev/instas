# Design Engineering Philosophy & Etiquette

## 1. The Design Engineer Role
*   **Death of the Handoff:** The traditional model of rigid role handoffs is where design quality goes to die. True design engineering requires caring as much about the final production implementation as the static mockup files.
*   **Code as a Medium:** True UI design cannot be completed entirely in Figma. Design engineers design with code, using interactive prototypes to gather richer feedback from live components in production.
*   **The Yin and Yang of Craft:** Design engineering balances detail-oriented polishers (sweating micro-interactions, custom transition curves, and animations) with systems-minded developers (building solid, modular, and maintainable component codebases).

## 2. Taste as a Trained Instinct
*   **Not Magic:** Taste is not a mystical gift; it is a rational, trained instinct.
*   **Surround Yourself with Excellence:** Calibrate your eye by finding respected creators in the industry, studying their work, and documenting exactly *why* their visual decisions succeed.
*   **Seek Feedback:** Push your work in public, share screenshots, and solicit expert critiques to align your visual execution with high-industry standards.

## 3. Software as a Gift
*   **The "Long, Hard, Stupid Way":** Genuine craft involves spending extra time and focus on micro-details far beyond the point of strict financial logic.
*   **Transfer of Enthusiasm:** This excessive care transfers enthusiasm from the maker to the audience, turning a functional digital tool into an empathetic "gift".

## 4. The Aesthetic-Usability Effect
*   **Aesthetic Buffer:** Users naturally perceive highly polished interfaces as more usable and show far more patience with minor layout or experience bugs.
*   **Usability Testing Caveat:** High visual polish can mask severe usability flaws during testing. Researchers must analyze user actions rather than merely taking positive aesthetic comments at face value.
